    The research

    The problem in AI

    Standard RAG treats memory as a flat bag of text chunks: embed everything, rank by cosine similarity, paste the top-k into the model. That has three structural failures: it loses structure (a fact and the concept it belongs to are just two equally-shaped vectors), it loses multi-hop reasoning (answering “the mentor of the director of film X” needs chains, not neighbors), and it wastes context on near-duplicate chunks.

    The hypothesis

    Inspired by the radial organization of mandalas — a center, concentric rings of decreasing generality, and semantic sectors — MIRA arranges memory as a formal topology: ring 0 core, ring 1 major concepts, ring 2 subconcepts, ring 3 facts/evidence, ring 4 source documents. Every node carries a computed radial distance (semantic + hierarchical + graph + temporal distance). Retrieval scores nine components, including spreading activation — energy that propagates through the graph the way activation spreads in associative memory (Collins & Loftus 1975).

    Honesty: MIRA is inspired by mandala organization principles and by biological memory dynamics. It does not claim mandalas were neural networks or that this simulates a brain. Whether radial organization helps is an empirical question — the system is built to answer it, including with “no”.

    Three biological timescales

    • Fast (per query) — spreading activation propagates energy through the mandala to surface multi-hop associates that vector search misses.
    • Medium (per answer) — nine-component scoring weighs semantic, structural, radial, graph, activation, confidence, recency and path evidence.
    • Slow (consolidation) — Hebbian plasticity: edges along successful retrieval paths strengthen, unused edges decay. The wiring adapts to use.

    What it is used for

    • A private, offline research memory over your own PDFs, notes and web pages — answers always cite document/page/chunk provenance.
    • An experimental laboratory: every mechanism (vector, graph, hierarchy, radial, activation) is individually switchable and benchmarkable.
    • A learning memory: a delta-rule unit learns the retrieval weights from your corpus; web-fetched pages become permanent memories; Hebbian consolidation rewires the graph toward what you actually ask.

    Measured so far (this installation, corpus-generated QA — sanity benchmark)

    • Trained neural scorer vs hand-tuned weights: MRR 0.365 → 0.488 (+34%); the unit learned that semantic (0.69) + activation (0.25) deserve most of the weight.
    • Agent routing verified: memory → cited answers; web escalation → live pages ingested as permanent memories; ungrounded parametric answers always labeled.
    • Topological upgrade: radial distance now computed for every node; rings follow §9 semantics (1 core · 19 major · 139 sub · 21 facts · 22 documents at time of writing).

    These are single-corpus sanity numbers, not headline claims. Run the Benchmarks page on real datasets (HotpotQA, MuSiQue) for publishable results; the paper draft lives in paper/mira_paper.md.

    What problem this solves in AI, concretely

    • Context efficiency — compression keeps only answer-critical evidence, so a 1.5B model on 4 GB VRAM behaves like a much larger one on its own corpus.
    • Auditable grounding — no answer without a retrieval path and sources; nothing is cited that was not retrieved.
    • Continual learning without fine-tuning — the memory grows (web ingest) and rewires (Hebbian) while the model stays frozen; external memory instead of expensive retraining.
    • Honest evaluation culture — ablations and baselines are first-class, so improvements are attributable to specific mechanisms.
